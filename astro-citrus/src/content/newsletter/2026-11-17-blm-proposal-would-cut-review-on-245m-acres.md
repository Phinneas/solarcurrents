# The fight to keep the public in public lands

**The Bottom Line:** The Bureau of Land Management, under the Trump administration, has proposed a rule that would gut public comment, environmental review, and Tribal consultation across **245 million acres** of Western public lands. The stated goal is to fast-track oil, gas, mining, and data center development. A coalition spanning Sierra Club, Earthjustice, NRDC, Defenders of Wildlife, and dozens of state-based conservation and Tribal-allied groups has come out against it. The public comment period is open now through [January 9](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1v1Wb_b21v9YkxUPKLGRMBXd9K9mYjfqOsHF6h1hheUQ/edit?tab=t.0).

**The Story:** The National Parks Conservation Association points to roughly 80 national park sites that share a border with BLM land, places where Areas of Critical Environmental Concern designations currently do real work: shielding Dinosaur National Monument from drilling impacts, protecting the Pinto Mountains next to Joshua Tree, and preserving ancestral sites near Hovenweep. Weakening ACEC protections doesn't stay contained to BLM land on a map. It reaches into the parks next door.

**By the numbers:**
- **245 million acres** of BLM-managed Western public land covered by the proposed rule, per [Sierra Club's analysis](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/11/10/trump-administration-continues-systematic-dismantling-of-public-lands/)
- Public comment period cut from the current 90 days down to **30 days** for governors, with no appeal, according to NRDC's Bobby McEnaney
- **60-day** total public comment window, closing **January 9, 2026**
- **96%** of Coloradans support conserving public lands and open space, while **81%** say oil and gas development is not an acceptable use of them, per Wilderness Workshop's Juli Slivka
- Roughly **80** national park units border BLM land that could lose ACEC protections, per the National Parks Conservation Association

**What remains:** The rule is a proposal, not yet final, and the 60-day comment window is the main lever the public has before January 9. It also lands alongside the rescission of the Public Lands Rule and threatened rollbacks to the Roadless Rule and NEPA standards. This fight is one front in a larger pattern, not an isolated decision.

# Montana becomes a test case for fast-tracked extraction

**The Bottom Line:** Montana signed a formal agreement with the federal Permitting Council in late October, becoming the 11th state to opt into the FAST-41 program, a fast-track review system now expanded under Trump to cover AI data centers and the mineral mining that feeds them. The deal lets state and federal agencies set permitting timelines together and gives select mining and energy projects a dedicated coordinator to push them through review faster. Three Montana mining projects, [Sheep Creek](https://www.permits.performance.gov/), Blue Copper, and the Stillwater Mine, already carry a FAST-41 "transparency" listing, the first step toward expedited status. [(Grist)](https://grist.org/politics/how-trump-plans-to-fast-track-mining-in-montana/)

**The Story:** Derf Johnson, Deputy Director of the Montana Environmental Information Center, doesn't object to the public dashboard FAST-41 creates. He calls it useful. What worries him is who decides which projects get the fast lane. "I think there's room for a lot of political interference, ignoring state law, and cutting the public out of the process," he told Grist. "I'm worried about projects receiving this designation in the age of corruption that we live in."

**By the numbers:**
- **11** states have now signed FAST-41 agreements with the federal Permitting Council, with Montana joining Alaska, Idaho, Wyoming, Nevada, Utah, Missouri, Tennessee, Louisiana, Georgia, and Iowa [(Grist)](https://grist.org/politics/how-trump-plans-to-fast-track-mining-in-montana/)
- **3** mining or mineral exploration projects in Montana already hold FAST-41 "transparency" status: Sheep Creek, Blue Copper, and Stillwater Mine [(Grist)](https://grist.org/politics/how-trump-plans-to-fast-track-mining-in-montana/)
- Trump's July 2025 executive order set the bar for "Qualifying Projects" at **$500 million** in committed capital spending or an electric load increase over **100 MW**, thresholds that pull large-scale mining and data center buildouts into the fast-track pipeline [(White House)](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/accelerating-federal-permitting-of-data-center-infrastructure/)
- This is the **first** time in FAST-41's decade-long history that states have entered direct agreements with the Permitting Council, making Montana's MOU an untested model [(Grist)](https://grist.org/politics/how-trump-plans-to-fast-track-mining-in-montana/)

**What remains:** FAST-41 doesn't rewrite environmental law the way BAAJA (the permitting bill the Senate passed this week) would. It only changes which projects get priority processing and a federal project coordinator. Whether Montana's agreement actually speeds anything up depends on staffing and funding for the scientists and legal reviewers who process applications. The Montana Environmental Information Center is still pushing to make sure state-level environmental review and the state's constitutional "clean and healthy environment" protections aren't quietly sidelined in the process.

# Veterans are quietly powering the solar workforce

**The Bottom Line:** Veterans make up roughly **8-9%** of the entire U.S. solar workforce, built through a patchwork of Department of Defense transition programs, GI Bill-funded certifications, and nonprofit training pipelines. [Airstreams Renewables](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/), [Solar Energy International](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/), [GRID Alternatives](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/), and NABCEP all run active pathways moving service members and their families directly into solar technician, installation, and project development roles.

**The Story:** Solar Energy International's Active Duty Military and Veterans Career Transition Program lets veterans use VA education benefits to complete its Solar Professionals Certificate Program at no out-of-pocket cost. Military spouses, often overlooked in workforce transition coverage, can access up to $4,000 through the MyCAA Scholarship Program to earn the same residential, commercial, or battery storage certificates. A family doesn't have to choose between the service member's transition and the spouse's career.

**By the numbers:**
- Veterans comprise **8% to 9%** of the total U.S. solar workforce, according to [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/)
- DoD SkillBridge lets transitioning troops train for solar careers during their final **180 days** of active duty while keeping full military pay, per [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/)
- Military spouses can draw up to **$4,000** through the MyCAA Scholarship Program toward solar certification, per [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/)
- Hiring Our Heroes runs **12-week** paid fellowships placing veterans directly into clean energy employers, per [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/)
- Residential installers like Watts for Warriors and Pro-Stat Solar Group offer veterans a flat **$1,000** discount on home solar installs, per [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/11/11/veterans-anchor-the-u-s-solar-workforce/)

**What remains:** These pathways aren't a unified federal workforce strategy. They depend on individual base commanders approving SkillBridge slots and nonprofit capacity to run certificate programs, not a guaranteed right of transition. Veterans in rural areas or at bases without active industry partnerships still face real gaps in access to these programs.

# Solarpunk Shelf: a new genre prize and a new indie bookstore

**The Bottom Line:** Nalo Hopkinson won the inaugural [Andrew Pyper Prize for Genre Fiction](https://www.writerstrust.com/awards/andrew-pyper-prize-for-genre-fiction), a $20,000 CAD award from the Writers' Trust of Canada honoring genre storytelling. Meanwhile, in Waunakee, Wisconsin, two founders are opening [Soulbound Books](https://www.soulboundbooks.com/), a new independent bookstore specializing in science fiction and fantasy, and the town's only indie bookstore. Neither story is about solar panels or policy, but both are about who gets to imagine other worlds, and where.

**The Story:** Hopkinson has spent decades writing Afrofuturist and Caribbean-inflected speculative fiction that centers Black, queer, and diasporic characters building and surviving new worlds; readers unfamiliar with her work can start with *Brown Girl in the Ring* or *Midnight Robber*. In Waunakee, founders Elizabeth Arpin and Katie Rittenhouse said they got excited realizing there wasn't another bookstore within a dozen miles of downtown. The bookstore opens in December 2026 and is running pop-up events in the meantime.

**Why it matters:** Solarpunk is a genre of imagination before it's a genre of infrastructure. Writers like Hopkinson have spent years modeling what survival, community, and power look like outside extractive systems. Independent bookstores like Soulbound do the unglamorous work of putting those stories into readers' hands, in person, in towns that don't have another option.

**What remains:** One prize and one bookstore don't fix a shrinking independent bookselling market or guarantee speculative fiction a wide audience. What they do is prove the appetite is still there, in Toronto jury rooms and in a Wisconsin storefront alike.

## The Quick Scribbles

**Austria's** [launched](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/11/11/austrias-solar-and-storage-rebates-exhausted-in-29-seconds/) solar and storage rebate program this week, and the entire fund was claimed in just 29 seconds flat. Household demand for clean energy independence is still massively outpacing available subsidy dollars.

**Melbourne's** [completed](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/11/11/cbus-completes-solar-skin-to-help-power-new-48-storey-office-tower/) one of Australia's first large-scale building-integrated solar facades, wrapping a new 48-storey office tower in nearly 1,400 panels that double as the building's skin. A glimpse of what solarpunk skylines could actually look like.

**Saudi Arabia** [opened](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/11/11/saudi-arabia-launches-1-9-gw-pv-tender/) bidding on a massive 1.9 GW solar tender, another sign that utility-scale solar economics have become too compelling for even petrostates to ignore.

**Australia** [cancelled](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/11/12/federal-government-cancels-panel-recycling-pilot-considers-next-steps/) its search for an administrator to run a National Solar Panel Recycling Pilot, a reminder that the clean energy transition still has real gaps to close around what happens to panels at the end of their life.
