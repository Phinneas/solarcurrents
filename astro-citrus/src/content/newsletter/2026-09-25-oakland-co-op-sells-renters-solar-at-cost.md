# Oakland nonprofit proves renters can access solar through co-op power

**The Bottom Line:** [Bright Saver](https://www.brightsaver.org/), an Oakland nonprofit, sells plug-in balcony solar kits to renters at cost through a member co-op. No markup. $285.30 for a 180W system plus $29 annual membership. After [hitting 248% of its crowdfunding goal](https://solarbuildermag.com/residential-solar/bright-saver-launches-second-balcony-solar-crowdfunding-effort/) in June 2025, the co-op is expanding to batteries and heat pumps. This is mutual aid meets clean energy: collective purchasing power that bypasses both landlords and retail extractors.

**The Story:** The model turns membership into market power. Bright Saver buys panels in bulk, passes savings directly to members, and uses growing membership numbers to pressure manufacturers and lawmakers. Co-CEO Cora Stryker puts it plainly: "We've already shown that people want this. Now the opportunity is to scale it."

**By the numbers:**
- Bright Saver charges **$285.30** for a 180W plug-in solar system, the actual cost with zero markup
- Annual membership costs **$29**, funding advocacy work and unlocking at-cost pricing
- The nonprofit's first crowdfunding campaign closed at **248%** of target in June 2025
- **35 states** have introduced legislation to legalize plug-in solar systems
- **44 million** U.S. renter households represent the potential market

**What remains:** The co-op model only works if membership grows fast enough to sustain bulk purchasing power. Manufacturers need to see consistent volume to maintain pricing. Whether collective buying can scale to serve millions of renters without losing the cost advantage is the test.

# Albuquerque deploys 6 MW of solar it won't own for 25 years

**The Bottom Line:** Albuquerque broke ground on **6 MW** of solar across eight municipal facilities to close the gap toward 100% renewable energy for city operations. But [Sustainability Partners](https://www.sustainability.partners/), a private Public Benefit Company, owns the infrastructure for **25 years** while the city makes monthly usage payments. Mayor Tim Keller frames it as using "every tool available" to deploy clean energy without upfront capital costs. The arrangement raises questions about who profits from publicly-sited renewable generation and whether municipal bond financing would serve taxpayers better than private lease payments.

**The Story:** Construction began last week at Highland Pool and the Eastside Animal Shelter, the first of eight city facilities getting solar arrays and carports through 2027. The city touts avoiding "the entire cost of building and maintaining these systems." But they haven't disclosed the total cost of **25 years** of monthly payments to private investors, making it impossible to compare against public financing options like municipal bonds that would give the city ownership from day one.

**By the numbers:**
- **6 MW** of solar generation across eight facilities: two aquatic centers, two animal/recreation facilities, Isotopes Park stadium, a transit center, the 911 call center, and Albuquerque International Sunport, per [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/09/eight-solar-projects-should-bring-albuquerque-to-100-renewable-energy/)
- Sunport installation includes **10 EV chargers** and battery storage for critical electrical backup, per [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/09/eight-solar-projects-should-bring-albuquerque-to-100-renewable-energy/)
- Construction phases through **2027** with Energy Systems Group handling installation, per [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/09/eight-solar-projects-should-bring-albuquerque-to-100-renewable-energy/)
- Total 25-year cost to taxpayers: **not disclosed**

**What remains:** The question isn't whether Albuquerque gets solar. It does. It's whether treating public solar infrastructure like a utility bill rather than a community asset serves the public interest, or whether municipal bonds and direct ownership would give ratepayers better long-term value and democratic control over their energy future.

# Germany threatens to kill its solar industry (again)

**The Bottom Line:** Germany's solar installers are having their best year in 2026, but the government is preparing to repeat the policy disaster that killed half its solar companies between 2012 and 2014. Proposed regulations from Minister Katherina Reiche cap grid feed-in at 50% with no transitional measures. The threat alone has already triggered company failures and financing freezes. Major project developer [Enerparc has filed for bankruptcy](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/18/regulatory-uncertainty-threatens-to-slow-german-pv-deployment-in-2027/), battery manufacturer RCT Power has followed, and banks are tightening terms on existing solar loans while rejecting new applications.

**The Story:** Installation companies report order books full through year-end as German customers rush to commission systems before the law passes. The urgency isn't theoretical. Many projects that would be economically viable under current Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG) terms face losses if completed after the feed-in cap takes effect. The rush has created artificial demand strong enough to mask the financing crisis building underneath, as banks withdraw from a sector losing its regulatory backstop.

**By the numbers:**

- Between 2012 and 2014, subsidy cuts championed by then-Environment Minister Peter Altmaier drove **more than half** of Germany's solar companies into insolvency in what became known as the "Altmaier effect" ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/18/regulatory-uncertainty-threatens-to-slow-german-pv-deployment-in-2027/))
- Proposed regulations cap grid feed-in at **50%** of system capacity with no transitional provisions for projects already in development ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/18/regulatory-uncertainty-threatens-to-slow-german-pv-deployment-in-2027/))
- Banks are rejecting new solar financing applications and tightening terms on existing loans as regulatory uncertainty removes the security the EEG historically provided ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/18/regulatory-uncertainty-threatens-to-slow-german-pv-deployment-in-2027/))

**What remains:** This isn't technical policy refinement. It's protecting fossil fuel utilities from what the government views as "inconvenient decentralization." The bills haven't passed the Bundestag yet, but without transitional measures for projects already in development, the insolvencies already announced will be only the beginning of another industry-wide collapse.

# Green hydrogen moves from pilot to production at world's largest chemical plant

**The Bottom Line:** BASF and Siemens Energy are integrating green electricity-powered electrolyzers into BASF's Ludwigshafen facility, the world's largest contiguous chemical production site. This is industrial decarbonization shifting from pilot projects to commercial deployment. The chemical industry requires massive hydrogen volumes for ammonia, methanol, and plastics manufacturing. This installation shows how industrial giants operate electrolyzers that continuously adjust to supply and demand in real-time production environments.

**The Story:** At Ludwigshafen, where 33,000 employees work across interconnected production units, the electrolyzer must balance grid renewable availability against the plant's constant demand for hydrogen feedstock. The operation requires continuous adjustment: ramping production when solar and wind power surge, throttling back during grid constraints, while maintaining the steady hydrogen supply that chemical manufacturing depends on.

**By the numbers:**
- The Ludwigshafen site is the world's largest contiguous chemical production facility, employing approximately **33,000** people ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/19/green-hydrogens-growing-share-in-chemical-production/))
- *Note: Specific electrolyzer capacity, production volumes, and investment data are not available in accessible source materials*

**What remains:** The project's full technical specifications (electrolyzer capacity, annual hydrogen production targets, integration timeline) are not publicly available. More critically, whether corporate-led industrial decarbonization can scale fast enough without stronger policy mandates remains the central question for heavy industry's climate impact.
