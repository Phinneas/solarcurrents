Balcony solar (also called plug-in solar) is exactly what it sounds like: 1–3 panels that mount on a [balcony railing](/posts/balcony-solar-panels/), patio fence, or exterior wall, connected to a small microinverter, [plugged into a standard wall outlet](/notes/why-balcony-solar-has-a-genuine-safety-consideration-worth-knowing/). No installer. No permits. No roof.

The concept has been common in Germany for years — roughly 40% of new solar registrations there in 2024 were balcony systems. The US is catching up, slowly.

[Utah](/posts/utah-hb-340-2025-explained/) was the first state to explicitly legalize it in March 2025. Maine followed in April 2026. Virginia is close. Around 30 other states have bills in progress.

A typical 400–800W system costs $500–$1,500 and can offset roughly $15–$50 per month off an electricity bill depending on location and rates. Not transformative — but real savings for people who previously had no solar options at all.

The catch: in states without enabling legislation, utilities often treat a single plug-in panel the same as a [full rooftop array](/posts/balcony-solar-laws-by-state/) — requiring interconnection agreements, fees, and weeks of waiting. Check your state before buying.
